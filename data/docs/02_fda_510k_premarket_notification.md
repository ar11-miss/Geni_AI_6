# FDA 510(k) Premarket Notification

## Purpose and Legal Basis
A 510(k) is a premarket submission made to FDA to demonstrate that a device to be marketed is at least as safe and effective as, that is, substantially equivalent (SE) to, a legally marketed device (the "predicate") that is not subject to PMA. Requirements are in section 510(k) of the FD&C Act and 21 CFR Part 807 Subpart E. A 510(k) must be submitted at least 90 days before introducing the device into commercial distribution, and the device may not be marketed until the manufacturer receives an order from FDA declaring it substantially equivalent ("510(k) clearance").

## Substantial Equivalence
A device is substantially equivalent if, compared to a predicate, it (1) has the same intended use, and (2) has the same technological characteristics; or has different technological characteristics that do not raise different questions of safety and effectiveness, and the information submitted demonstrates the device is as safe and effective as the predicate. Performance data may include bench testing, biocompatibility, electrical safety and EMC, software documentation, sterilization validation, and in some cases clinical data.

## Types of 510(k)
- Traditional 510(k): the standard pathway, usable in any situation.
- Special 510(k): for a change to the manufacturer's own legally marketed device where the method to evaluate the change is well established and results can be reviewed in a summary or risk analysis format. FDA's goal is to review Special 510(k)s within 30 days.
- Abbreviated 510(k): relies on FDA guidance documents, special controls, or FDA-recognized consensus standards to demonstrate equivalence, often using summary reports.

## Review Process and Timelines
Under the MDUFA performance goals, FDA aims to make a decision on a Traditional 510(k) within 90 FDA days (the clock pauses while the submitter responds to questions). Within 15 calendar days of receipt, FDA performs an acceptance review using the Refuse to Accept (RTA) checklist. If FDA issues an Additional Information (AI) request, the review is placed on hold and the submitter has 180 calendar days to respond; otherwise the submission is considered withdrawn. Since October 1, 2023, all 510(k) submissions must be submitted electronically using the eSTAR (electronic Submission Template And Resource) template, unless exempted. A user fee is payable for each 510(k), with reduced fees for qualifying small businesses.

## When a New 510(k) Is Required for a Change
Under 21 CFR 807.81(a)(3), a new 510(k) is required when a change or modification to a cleared device could significantly affect its safety or effectiveness (for example, a change in design, material, chemical composition, energy source, or manufacturing process), or when there is a major change or modification in the intended use. FDA's guidance "Deciding When to Submit a 510(k) for a Change to an Existing Device" (2017) and the companion guidance for software changes provide decision flowcharts. Changes that do not require a new 510(k) must still be documented under design controls. A Predetermined Change Control Plan (PCCP) authorized in a marketing submission can allow specified future modifications without a new submission.

## Third Party Review
FDA's 510(k) Third Party Review Program allows accredited review organizations to review certain eligible 510(k)s and make a recommendation to FDA, which may shorten review time.
