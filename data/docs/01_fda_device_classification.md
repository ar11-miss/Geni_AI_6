# FDA Medical Device Classification

## Overview
The U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) regulates medical devices under the Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act (FD&C Act). Every device is assigned to one of three regulatory classes based on the level of control necessary to provide reasonable assurance of its safety and effectiveness. Classification procedures are described in 21 CFR Part 860, and the device classification regulations themselves are organized into 16 medical specialty "panels" in 21 CFR Parts 862 through 892 (for example, Part 870 covers cardiovascular devices and Part 880 covers general hospital and personal use devices).

## Class I – General Controls
Class I devices present the lowest risk. They are subject only to general controls, which apply to all devices: establishment registration and device listing (21 CFR Part 807), prohibitions against adulteration and misbranding, labeling requirements (21 CFR Part 801), medical device reporting (21 CFR Part 803), and quality management system requirements (21 CFR Part 820), unless specifically exempted. Most Class I devices are exempt from premarket notification (510(k)). Examples include elastic bandages, examination gloves and manual stethoscopes.

## Class II – Special Controls
Class II devices are moderate risk. General controls alone are insufficient, so they are also subject to special controls, which can include performance standards, device-specific guidance documents, special labeling requirements, and postmarket surveillance. Most Class II devices require a 510(k) premarket notification demonstrating substantial equivalence to a legally marketed predicate. Examples include powered wheelchairs, infusion pumps (product code FRN, 21 CFR 880.5725) and surgical drapes.

## Class III – Premarket Approval
Class III devices are the highest risk. They usually sustain or support life, are implanted, or present a potential unreasonable risk of illness or injury. General and special controls are insufficient, so most Class III devices require Premarket Approval (PMA) under 21 CFR Part 814. Examples include implantable pacemakers, implantable cardioverter defibrillators and replacement heart valves.

## Product Codes and Finding a Classification
Each generic device type is identified by a three-letter product code and a classification regulation number. Manufacturers can find the classification by searching the FDA Product Classification Database, or by identifying a similar legally marketed device. When classification is unclear, a manufacturer may submit a 513(g) Request for Information to obtain FDA's view on the classification and regulatory requirements applicable to a device.

## Novel Devices
Devices that have no legally marketed predicate are automatically placed into Class III by operation of law. If such a device is low to moderate risk, the sponsor can request classification into Class I or II through the De Novo pathway rather than pursuing a PMA.
