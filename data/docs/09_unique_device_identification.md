# Unique Device Identification (UDI)

## U.S. UDI System
FDA's UDI final rule, published September 24, 2013, established a system to adequately identify medical devices through distribution and use. Requirements are in 21 CFR Part 801 Subpart B (labeling) and 21 CFR Part 830 (UDI). Device labelers must place a UDI on the label and packages of devices, and in some cases directly mark the device itself when it is intended to be used more than once and reprocessed between uses. Labelers must submit device information to FDA's Global Unique Device Identification Database (GUDID), which is publicly accessible through AccessGUDID.

## Structure of a UDI
A UDI is a unique numeric or alphanumeric code with two parts:
- Device Identifier (DI): a mandatory, fixed portion that identifies the labeler and the specific version or model of the device.
- Production Identifier (PI): a conditional, variable portion that identifies one or more of the following when included on the label: the lot or batch number, the serial number, the expiration date, the date of manufacture, and, for HCT/Ps regulated as devices, the distinct identification code.
The UDI must be presented in both a plain-text (human-readable) form and a form using automatic identification and data capture (AIDC) technology, such as a linear barcode, 2D barcode (e.g., Data Matrix) or RFID.

## Issuing Agencies
UDIs must be issued under a system operated by an FDA-accredited issuing agency. FDA has accredited three issuing agencies: GS1, the Health Industry Business Communications Council (HIBCC), and ICCBBA (the agency for ISBT 128, used for blood, cells, tissues and organs).

## EU UDI
Under the EU MDR, devices are assigned a UDI-DI and UDI-PI and additionally a Basic UDI-DI, which is the primary identifier of a device model used in EUDAMED, certificates, the EU declaration of conformity and the technical documentation. The Basic UDI-DI does not appear on the device label.
