# Quality Management Systems: FDA QMSR and ISO 13485

## The Quality Management System Regulation (QMSR)
FDA's device quality system requirements are found in 21 CFR Part 820. On February 2, 2024 FDA published a final rule amending Part 820 to become the Quality Management System Regulation (QMSR), which incorporates by reference the international standard ISO 13485:2016, "Medical devices – Quality management systems – Requirements for regulatory purposes". The QMSR became effective on February 2, 2026, replacing the former Quality System Regulation (QSR) that had been in place since 1996. The QMSR includes supplemental provisions where FDA retained or clarified requirements, for example control of records (820.35), which addresses complaint and servicing records and Unique Device Identifier (UDI) information, and controls for labeling and packaging (820.45). With the QMSR, FDA stated it would no longer use the Quality System Inspection Technique (QSIT) and adopted an updated inspection approach aligned with ISO 13485.

## Structure of ISO 13485:2016
ISO 13485:2016 is organized into clauses: 4 Quality management system (including documentation, the medical device file and control of records); 5 Management responsibility (quality policy, objectives, management review); 6 Resource management (personnel competence, infrastructure, work environment); 7 Product realization (planning, customer-related processes, design and development, purchasing, production and service provision); and 8 Measurement, analysis and improvement (feedback, complaint handling, reporting to regulatory authorities, internal audit, control of nonconforming product, analysis of data, corrective action and preventive action). Risk-based approaches are applied throughout the QMS, and records must generally be retained for at least the lifetime of the device as defined by the manufacturer, but not less than two years from release.

## Design and Development Controls
Design controls (ISO 13485 clause 7.3) apply to the development of devices and include: design and development planning; design inputs (functional, performance, usability and safety requirements, applicable regulatory requirements, and outputs of risk management); design outputs (specifications, drawings, software, labeling); design reviews at suitable stages; design verification (confirming outputs meet inputs, e.g., by testing); design validation (confirming the device meets user needs and intended uses, performed on initial production units or equivalents and including clinical evaluation or performance evaluation, and usability where applicable); design transfer to production; and control of design changes. The design history and records are maintained in a design and development file (historically called the Design History File, DHF, under the QSR).

## Corrective and Preventive Action (CAPA)
Corrective action (ISO 13485 clause 8.5.2) eliminates the cause of existing nonconformities to prevent recurrence; preventive action (8.5.3) eliminates the cause of potential nonconformities. The documented procedure must cover reviewing nonconformities including complaints, determining causes, evaluating the need for action, planning and documenting action, verifying that the action does not adversely affect the ability to meet regulatory requirements or the safety and performance of the device, and reviewing the effectiveness of the action taken. CAPA inputs include complaints, nonconforming product, internal and external audits, service records, process monitoring and trend data.

## Complaint Handling
ISO 13485 clause 8.2.2 requires documented procedures for timely complaint handling, including receiving and recording information, evaluating whether the feedback is a complaint, investigating complaints, determining the need to report to regulatory authorities, handling of related product, and determining the need to initiate corrections or corrective actions. If a complaint is not investigated, the justification must be documented.
