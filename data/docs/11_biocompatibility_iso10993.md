# Biocompatibility Evaluation – ISO 10993-1

## Principles
ISO 10993-1, "Biological evaluation of medical devices – Part 1: Evaluation and testing within a risk management process", requires the biological evaluation of a device to be planned and performed as part of the ISO 14971 risk management process. Evaluation begins with characterization of materials, manufacturing processes and existing data; testing is performed only where existing information is insufficient. FDA's guidance "Use of International Standard ISO 10993-1" (updated September 2023) describes FDA's expectations, including a biocompatibility evaluation endpoints matrix.

## Categorization by Nature of Body Contact
- Surface devices: contact with intact skin, mucosal membranes, or breached or compromised surfaces.
- Externally communicating devices: contact with the blood path indirectly, tissue/bone/dentin, or circulating blood.
- Implant devices: contact with tissue/bone or blood.

## Categorization by Duration of Contact
- Limited exposure (A): cumulative contact of 24 hours or less.
- Prolonged exposure (B): cumulative contact of more than 24 hours up to 30 days.
- Long-term exposure (C): cumulative contact of more than 30 days.

## Biological Endpoints
Endpoints to consider depend on contact category and duration. Cytotoxicity (ISO 10993-5), sensitization (ISO 10993-10) and irritation (ISO 10993-23), sometimes called the "big three", are relevant to almost all patient-contacting devices. Other endpoints include acute systemic toxicity and subacute/subchronic/chronic toxicity (ISO 10993-11), material-mediated pyrogenicity, genotoxicity (ISO 10993-3), implantation (ISO 10993-6), hemocompatibility (ISO 10993-4) for blood-contacting devices, and carcinogenicity. Chemical characterization (ISO 10993-18) combined with toxicological risk assessment (ISO 10993-17) is increasingly used to address systemic endpoints and reduce animal testing.
