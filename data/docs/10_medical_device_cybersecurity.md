# Medical Device Cybersecurity

## Section 524B of the FD&C Act
The Consolidated Appropriations Act, 2023 (signed December 29, 2022) added section 524B, "Ensuring Cybersecurity of Devices", to the FD&C Act, effective March 29, 2023. It applies to a "cyber device", meaning a device that (1) includes software validated, installed or authorized by the sponsor as a device or in a device; (2) has the ability to connect to the internet; and (3) contains any technological characteristics validated, installed or authorized by the sponsor that could be vulnerable to cybersecurity threats.

## Required Content of Premarket Submissions
Sponsors of a premarket submission (510(k), PMA, De Novo, PDP or HDE) for a cyber device must:
1. Submit a plan to monitor, identify and address, as appropriate, in a reasonable time, postmarket cybersecurity vulnerabilities and exploits, including coordinated vulnerability disclosure and related procedures;
2. Design, develop and maintain processes and procedures to provide a reasonable assurance that the device and related systems are cybersecure, and make available postmarket updates and patches to the device and related systems — on a reasonably justified regular cycle for known unacceptable vulnerabilities, and as soon as possible out of cycle for critical vulnerabilities that could cause uncontrolled risks;
3. Provide a Software Bill of Materials (SBOM), including commercial, open-source and off-the-shelf software components;
4. Comply with other requirements FDA may establish by regulation.
FDA began refusing to accept (RTA) premarket submissions for cyber devices lacking this information on October 1, 2023.

## FDA Premarket Cybersecurity Guidance
FDA's guidance "Cybersecurity in Medical Devices: Quality System Considerations and Content of Premarket Submissions" was finalized in September 2023 and updated in June 2025 to address section 524B. It recommends a Secure Product Development Framework (SPDF), threat modeling, a cybersecurity risk assessment separate from but linked to the ISO 14971 safety risk assessment (exploitability rather than probability is assessed), security architecture views, cybersecurity testing (including penetration testing, fuzz testing and vulnerability scanning), unresolved anomaly assessment, and labeling describing security controls and the device's end-of-support date. Relevant standards and reports include AAMI TIR57 (security risk management), ANSI/AAMI SW96 and IEC 81001-5-1 (security activities in the product life cycle).
