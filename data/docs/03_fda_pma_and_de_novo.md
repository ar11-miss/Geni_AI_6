# FDA Premarket Approval (PMA) and De Novo Classification

## Premarket Approval (PMA)
PMA is the most stringent type of device marketing application, required for most Class III devices. It is governed by section 515 of the FD&C Act and 21 CFR Part 814. A PMA must contain sufficient valid scientific evidence to provide reasonable assurance that the device is safe and effective for its intended use. Unlike a 510(k), PMA approval is based on FDA's independent determination of safety and effectiveness rather than comparison to a predicate, and typically requires clinical data from an investigation conducted under an Investigational Device Exemption (IDE, 21 CFR Part 812).

## PMA Review Timeline
By regulation, FDA has 180 days to review a PMA. Under MDUFA goals, FDA aims to issue a decision within 180 FDA days for an original PMA that does not require an advisory committee (panel) meeting, and within 320 FDA days when a panel meeting is required. FDA typically performs a preapproval inspection of the manufacturing facilities for quality system compliance before approval.

## PMA Supplements and Postapproval Obligations
Changes to a PMA-approved device that affect safety or effectiveness require a PMA supplement. Types include panel-track supplements (significant changes needing clinical data), 180-day supplements (significant design or labeling changes), real-time supplements (minor changes reviewed in a meeting), special PMA supplements – changes being effected (labeling or manufacturing changes that enhance safety), and 30-day notices (manufacturing method changes). PMA holders must submit annual (periodic) reports and may be required to conduct post-approval studies as a condition of approval.

## De Novo Classification Request
The De Novo pathway provides a route to market for novel devices of low to moderate risk for which there is no legally marketed predicate. The requirements are codified in 21 CFR Part 860 Subpart D (final rule effective January 3, 2022). A sponsor may submit a De Novo request directly, or after receiving a Not Substantially Equivalent (NSE) determination on a 510(k). The request must include a proposed classification, a description of the risks to health, and proposed general and, if Class II, special controls that mitigate those risks, supported by data.

## De Novo Outcome and Timeline
Under MDUFA goals, FDA aims to decide on De Novo requests within 150 FDA review days. When a De Novo request is granted, FDA creates a new classification regulation for the device type, and the device may then serve as a predicate for future 510(k) submissions. De Novo submissions have also transitioned to the eSTAR electronic format.

## Breakthrough Devices Program
The Breakthrough Devices Program is a voluntary program for devices that provide more effective treatment or diagnosis of life-threatening or irreversibly debilitating diseases or conditions. It offers interactive and timely communication with FDA and prioritized review for 510(k), De Novo and PMA submissions, but does not change the statutory standard for marketing authorization.
