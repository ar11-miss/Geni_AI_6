# EU Medical Device Regulation (EU) 2017/745 (MDR)

## Overview
The EU Medical Device Regulation (EU) 2017/745 replaced the Medical Device Directive 93/42/EEC and the Active Implantable Medical Device Directive 90/385/EEC. It entered into force in May 2017 and has applied since 26 May 2021. Compared with the directives it strengthens requirements for clinical evidence, notified body oversight, post-market surveillance, traceability (UDI and the EUDAMED database), and transparency. Manufacturers must have at least one Person Responsible for Regulatory Compliance (PRRC) according to Article 15, and must affix the CE mark after completing the applicable conformity assessment procedure.

## Classification under Annex VIII
Devices are classified as Class I, IIa, IIb or III using the 22 classification rules in Annex VIII, which take into account duration of contact (transient: under 60 minutes; short-term: 60 minutes to 30 days; long-term: more than 30 days), invasiveness, whether the device is active, and the part of the body affected. Class I devices that are sterile (Is), have a measuring function (Im) or are reusable surgical instruments (Ir) require notified body involvement for those aspects. All other Class I devices are self-certified by the manufacturer.

## Rule 11 – Software
Rule 11 classifies software. Software intended to provide information which is used to take decisions with diagnosis or therapeutic purposes is classified as Class IIa, except where such decisions have an impact that may cause death or an irreversible deterioration of a person's state of health (Class III) or a serious deterioration of a person's state of health or a surgical intervention (Class IIb). Software intended to monitor physiological processes is Class IIa, except where it monitors vital physiological parameters where variations could result in immediate danger to the patient (Class IIb). All other software is Class I. MDCG 2019-11 provides guidance on qualification and classification of software.

## Transition Timelines
Regulation (EU) 2023/607 extended the MDR transition period for devices covered by valid certificates or declarations under the old directives: until 31 December 2027 for Class III devices and Class IIb implantable devices (with certain exceptions such as sutures, staples and dental fillings), and until 31 December 2028 for other Class IIb devices, Class IIa devices, and Class I devices that are sterile or have a measuring function. The extension applies only if conditions are met, including that the manufacturer had put in place an MDR-compliant QMS by 26 May 2024, had lodged a formal application with a notified body by 26 May 2024, and had signed a written agreement with the notified body by 26 September 2024, and that there are no significant changes in design or intended purpose.

## Post-Market Surveillance and Reporting
Manufacturers must plan and operate a post-market surveillance (PMS) system proportionate to the risk class (Articles 83–86). Class I manufacturers prepare a PMS report, updated when necessary. Class IIa, IIb and III manufacturers prepare a Periodic Safety Update Report (PSUR): for Class IIa devices updated when necessary and at least every two years; for Class IIb and Class III devices at least annually. For Class III and implantable devices, the PSUR is submitted via EUDAMED to the notified body for review. Post-Market Clinical Follow-up (PMCF) under Annex XIV Part B is used to proactively collect clinical data. For implantable devices and Class III devices, a Summary of Safety and Clinical Performance (SSCP) must be published (Article 32).

## Vigilance – Serious Incident Reporting (Article 87)
Manufacturers must report any serious incident involving their devices on the EU market to the relevant competent authority immediately after establishing a causal relationship (or that such a relationship is reasonably possible), and not later than 15 days after becoming aware of the incident. In the event of a serious public health threat the report must be provided immediately and not later than 2 days after awareness. In the event of death or an unanticipated serious deterioration in a person's state of health, the report must be provided immediately and not later than 10 days after awareness. Field Safety Corrective Actions (FSCAs) must be reported before they are undertaken (except in urgent cases) and communicated to users via a Field Safety Notice (FSN). Statistically significant increases in non-serious incidents or expected side-effects are subject to trend reporting (Article 88).

## EUDAMED
EUDAMED is the European database on medical devices, with modules for actor registration, UDI/device registration, notified bodies and certificates, clinical investigations and performance studies, vigilance and post-market surveillance, and market surveillance. Regulation (EU) 2024/1860 amended the MDR to allow a gradual, module-by-module roll-out with mandatory use of each module once its functionality has been confirmed.
