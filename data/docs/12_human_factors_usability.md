# Human Factors and Usability Engineering

## Standards and Guidance
IEC 62366-1:2015+A1:2020 specifies a usability engineering process for manufacturers to analyze, specify, develop and evaluate the usability of a medical device as it relates to safety. FDA's guidance "Applying Human Factors and Usability Engineering to Medical Devices" (February 2016) describes FDA's expectations, and FDA has issued draft guidance on the content of human factors information in marketing submissions.

## Key Concepts
- Use error: a user action or lack of action that leads to a different result than intended by the manufacturer or expected by the user.
- Critical task: a user task which, if performed incorrectly or not performed at all, would or could cause serious harm to the patient or user, where harm is defined to include compromised medical care.
- Use specification: summary of intended medical indication, patient population, intended users, use environment and operating principle.
- Use-related risk analysis (URRA): identifies tasks, potential use errors, resulting harms and their severity, and risk control measures.

## Formative and Summative Evaluation
Formative evaluations are conducted iteratively during design to identify user interface strengths, weaknesses and unanticipated use errors. Summative evaluation, called human factors validation testing by FDA, is performed on the final (production-equivalent) user interface with representative users performing critical tasks in a simulated or actual use environment, to demonstrate the device can be used safely and effectively. FDA recommends that human factors validation testing include a minimum of 15 participants from each distinct user group. Results include analysis of use errors, close calls and difficulties, with root-cause analysis informed by participant interviews.
