# ISO 14971:2019 – Application of Risk Management to Medical Devices

## Scope and Key Definitions
ISO 14971:2019 specifies a process for a manufacturer to identify hazards associated with medical devices (including in vitro diagnostic devices and software), estimate and evaluate the associated risks, control these risks, and monitor the effectiveness of the controls throughout the device life cycle. ISO/TR 24971:2020 provides guidance on its application. In the EU it is harmonized as EN ISO 14971:2019+A11:2021.
- Harm: injury or damage to the health of people, or damage to property or the environment.
- Hazard: a potential source of harm.
- Hazardous situation: a circumstance in which people, property or the environment are exposed to one or more hazards.
- Risk: the combination of the probability of occurrence of harm and the severity of that harm.
- Residual risk: risk remaining after risk control measures have been implemented.

## The Risk Management Process
The process (clauses 4–10) comprises: general requirements including a risk management plan, top management responsibilities, competence of personnel, and a risk management file (clause 4); risk analysis — intended use and reasonably foreseeable misuse, identification of characteristics related to safety, identification of hazards and hazardous situations, and risk estimation (clause 5); risk evaluation against the criteria for risk acceptability defined in the plan (clause 6); risk control (clause 7); evaluation of overall residual risk (clause 8); risk management review before commercial release, documented in a risk management report (clause 9); and production and post-production activities, i.e., collecting and reviewing information from production, post-market surveillance, and the state of the art (clause 10).

## Risk Control Option Analysis
ISO 14971 requires risk control options to be applied in the following priority order:
1. Inherent safety by design and manufacture;
2. Protective measures in the medical device itself or in the manufacturing process (for example, alarms, guards, or interlocks);
3. Information for safety and, where appropriate, training of users (for example, warnings in the instructions for use).
For each risk control measure the manufacturer must verify implementation and effectiveness, and evaluate whether new hazards or hazardous situations are introduced.

## Benefit-Risk Analysis
If a residual risk is not judged acceptable using the criteria in the risk management plan and further risk control is not practicable, the manufacturer may gather and review data to determine whether the medical benefits of the intended use outweigh that residual risk. If the benefits do not outweigh the risk, the risk remains unacceptable. The 2019 edition also requires disclosure of significant residual risks to users.

## Common Risk Analysis Techniques
Commonly used techniques include Preliminary Hazard Analysis (PHA), Failure Mode and Effects Analysis (FMEA, e.g., design FMEA and process FMEA), Fault Tree Analysis (FTA), Hazard and Operability Study (HAZOP) and Hazard Analysis and Critical Control Points (HACCP). Use-related risks are analyzed with a use-related risk analysis (URRA) according to IEC 62366-1, and security risks with approaches such as AAMI TIR57.
